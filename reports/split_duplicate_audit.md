# Do drawings of the same circuit straddle the split boundary? (mentor fix 1c)

Generated by `scripts/split_duplicate_audit.py`; numbers from
`results/split_duplicate_audit.json`.

## The headline

**75 of 192 test images (39.1%) have exactly the same circuit
topology as at least one validation image.**

Validation selects and test reports. A topology present in both means the
selection split contained the circuit the headline number is scored on. This is
measured, not inferred: a Weisfeiler-Lehman hash of the net/component graph,
invariant to net naming and to component id, over the 383 images that have
ground-truth netlists.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| images with GT netlists | 383 |
| distinct topologies among them | 255 |
| repeated-topology groups | 26 |
| groups spanning val and test | 19 |
| largest single group | 22 images |
| test images sharing a topology with **val** | 75 (39.1%) |
| test images sharing a topology with another **test** image | 72 |

255 distinct topologies across 383 annotated images means the corpus
repeats circuits heavily — the largest group is 22 drawings of one circuit.
An image-level split cannot help scattering those across splits, and it did.

## The train boundary cannot be measured, and that is the main limitation

The 895 training images have no netlist ground truth, so the exact test above
cannot be run against them. Nothing here proves a test circuit is topologically
absent from training. Closing that gap needs training-set netlists — an
annotation campaign, not a script.

What can be said: a corpus that repeats circuits this heavily across two splits
of 190 and 192 images is unlikely to stop doing so across the 895. The val/test
measurement makes train/test contamination **likely rather than speculative**,
which is a materially different claim from the one the paper currently makes.

## Two weaker measurements, reported with their limits

**Component inventory** (the multiset of component classes) is a *necessary* but
nowhere near sufficient condition for a duplicate. 63 inventory groups span
train and test out of 347 distinct signatures over 1277 images. This bounds
the risk from above and confirms nothing.

**Near-duplicate frames** — correlation of 64×64 ink-density vectors — found
2 pairs above 0.85, 0 of them across train and test. That null
result should **not** be taken as reassurance, because the measure was
calibrated against CGHD (which photographs each drawing four times, so the right
answer is known) and **it failed**: two photographs of the same sheet score
0.117 median against 0.054 for unrelated drawings, because the captures
differ in camera pose and a fixed-grid correlation cannot see through that. The
measure detects only well-registered duplicates. A hit would be real; a miss
proves nothing.

An 8×8 dHash was tried first and was worse: it reported 301 train/test
"near-duplicate" pairs whose actual pixel correlation was 0.08–0.43, i.e.
entirely unrelated circuits. These frames are mostly white paper, so a coarse
downsample is near-uniform for every drawing and the hashes collide en masse.
It is recorded here because it looked reasonable and was not.

## What to do about it

1. **Report the 39% figure.** It is the honest characterisation of the
   current split and a reviewer will find it if the paper does not.
2. **Re-split by topology group** for any future training run: assign whole
   groups to one split, exactly as `scripts/make_splits.py` already does for
   drafters when drafter metadata exists.
3. **Treat the val/test gap as a lower bound on optimism.** Model selection saw
   39% of the reported split's topologies.
