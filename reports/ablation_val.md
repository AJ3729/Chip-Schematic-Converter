## Why this exists

The reported ablation was computed on the **test** split. The mentor's first
review point asks that ablations and debugging run on validation, with the
hidden test touched only after the method is frozen. The arms here are replays
of frozen historical configurations rather than a selection procedure, so no
parameter was ever chosen using test — but the figure was still *reported* on
it, and that is what this replaces.

## What survives the move, and what does not

**The headline conclusion is robust.** Port templates are the largest single
contribution on both splits — **+0.2579 on validation** against
+0.3177 on test — and remain roughly three times the next largest,
ink-boundary snapping (+0.0842 val, +0.0833 test). The claim that the
biggest structural gain in the pipeline is a terminal-identity mechanism does
not depend on which split it is measured on.

**Crossover-aware junction resolution contributes exactly +0.0000 on validation
and +0.0000 on test.** The corrected claim from task A4 holds on both.

**Two smaller claims are split-specific and must be softened.** Bridge-span
widening is -0.0104 on test — the paper calls it "outright negative on strict
success" — but +0.0158 on validation. The port-head ensemble is +0.0260 on
test and +0.0000 on validation. Neither sign is stable across splits at
this sample size, and stating either as a finding overreads the data.

Stages whose sign flips between splits: + bridge span 7, + snap window 80, + Sauvola binarisation, + port-head ensemble (shipped).

## How to read the two columns

Validation is the number to quote for any claim about which stage helps, because
that is the split those decisions may legitimately consult. Test is shown beside
it as confirmation that the ordering is not an artifact of the split, not as the
primary evidence. The absolute levels differ (0.4474 val against
0.5312 test) in the direction the paper already reports for the main table.
